Abstract: Under anthropogenic greenhouse forcing, climate models project a decrease in year-to-year temperature variability in the mid-to-high latitudes and an increase in the tropics and subtropics. Here, we examine year-to-year temperature variability in the CESM Large Ensemble in concert with an idealized stochastic moist energy balance model (SMEBM) with effective surface heat capacity and local feedback parameter specified based on CESM. We find that the zonal-mean CESM variability change pattern largely persists after filtering out the impact of El Niño. The SMEBM largely captures this change pattern. In the SMEBM, changes in the mid-to-high latitudes are driven by changes in the effective heat capacity due to sea ice retreat, combined with changes in anomalous moist static energy convergence modulated by the polar-amplified time-mean temperature change. The CESM local feedback parameter evolves to be less stabilizing in the low latitudes, which causes tropical and subtropical variability to increase in the SMEBM.

Abstract: A tidal bore is a nonlinear step like surge propagating on the top of a river counter-current. The scientific literature reports that the passage of the tidal front may induce local flow reversals and, in some cases, a global reversal of the river current. Here, combining Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) measurements and 2D numerical simulations based on a Weakly Compressible Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (WCSPH) method, we show that the flow classification of tidal bores must explicitly take into account the frame of reference. When the river flow does not reverse after the bore passing, this is a consequence of the perspective imposed by the background current (itself resulting from the previous tide and the natural river discharge toward the sea). Depending on the relative magnitudes of the river current and the velocity field induced by the bore, one may observe a global reversal, a local reversal (or both), or no reversal at all. When the tide is spatially inhomogeneous, with a tidal range that varies along the estuary rather than a simple step in water depth, transitions between bore types may occur during the bore passing.

Abstract: Surface-related multiple elimination (SRME) is an indispensable processing step in marine seismic exploration, comprising two main components: multiple prediction and adaptive subtraction. While the multiple prediction is theoretically data-driven and relies on the data itself, the adaptive subtraction step requires a fairly subjective manual control. Our approach introduces a purely data-driven and physics-based deep learning (PDL) algorithm for improving the adaptive multiple subtraction to significantly enhanced performance. There are two main innovations in the proposed deep-learning (DL) multiple subtraction approaches: first, being data-driven and self-supervised, which removes the dependence on inflexible label preparation and model training processes; secondly, being governed by the adaptive matching filtering (AMF) equation to guide the training process. The DL module serves as a strong regression filter analogous to the traditionally known AMF. We segment a moderately sized seismic profile and an initial multiple model into a large number of patches using a patching scheme for self-contained training. The number of patches is controlled by the patch size and the shift size to enable a sufficient number of training samples required by mainstream DL platforms. The patching-based self-training and regression approach, PDL, is inherently a non-stationary matching approach that aims to obtain a globally optimal matching from the initial multiple models to the input full wavefield data through iterative patching-based training. It is also closely connected with the non-stationary regression approach, which was developed almost two decades ago. However, the PDL approach achieves even stronger regression performance due to the backpropagation-based optimization mechanism in DL, and has shown significantly improved multiple subtraction performance over a number of examples.

Abstract: Response-spectrum matching is an underdetermined waveform inverse problem in which spectral compatibility must be achieved without uncontrolled changes in other engineering-relevant ground-motion characteristics. This study formulates the problem as differentiable waveform inverse design using a fixed SDOF dynamics engine and configurable optimization modules. An envelope-guided parameterization defines the correction waveform, while a hard projection enforces zero correction-induced terminal velocity and displacement. Differentiable SDOF responses then provide gradients for waveform optimization. Spectral fidelity and Arias-intensity accumulation are treated as adaptive objectives. Spectral feasibility, PGA, total Arias intensity, and displacement-path requirements are incorporated as constraints. The differentiable spectrum operator agrees with an exact solution of the equation of motion to within 0.002%. On a common benchmark, maximum errors of 2.61% and 8.47% are obtained for single- and multi-damping matching, respectively, while retaining the additional waveform constraints.
